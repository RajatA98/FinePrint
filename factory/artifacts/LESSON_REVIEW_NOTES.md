## The finale

**Reconstruct — people.** Three cameos, named from memory: the niece (options niece / aunt / the
caller's sister), the lady of the house (same three), and the husband who walks in from the dusk
(options husband / Ronnie / the caller). Every decoy is a person in the story; `sister` is the strongest
one, because she is talked about constantly and was never in the room.

**Reconstruct — clues.** Seven lines, four proving. The proving four are the contradiction, the
arrival, the second invention and the narrator's verdict:

| clue | line | speaker | proving | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k1 | 24 | framton | no | true — he knows nobody here — but it proves nothing about the tragedy |
| k2 | 39 | vera | no | true — the window really is kept open — and it is the hook, not the proof |
| k3 | 71 | mrs-sappleton | **yes** | the aunt expects the same three men home today: the drowning never happened |
| k4 | 105 | narration | **yes** | the three men are on the lawn, in the narrator's own words |
| k5 | 113 | narration | no | true — he bolts — but a frightened man proves nothing about who lied |
| k6 | 126 | vera | **yes** | she invents a second story on the spot, for the same audience |
| k7 | 132 | narration | **yes** | the narrator names the habit: invention to order |

Speakers were assigned by whose mouth the words are in: k4, k5 and k7 are Saki's narration, not a
character's claim. That distinction is the taught content of the column.

**Culprit.** "Who made it happen?" — the niece. Options: niece, aunt, caller. The aunt is the decoy a
careless reader picks, because she is the one who says the men are coming; she is telling the truth.

**Statement.** `The girl began her account of the soft ground in a {slot1} voice, the three men came
back over the grass with one of them {slot2} under a white coat, and her tale about the dog was one
more {slot3} she produced on the spot.`

| slot | true | untrue options and why |
|---|---|---|
| slot1 | self-possessed | *ghastly* — the subject is ghastly to the caller, her manner is not; *dazed* — the dazed look is later, at the window, and is put on |
| slot2 | burdened | *engulfed* — nobody was engulfed; that is the invented drowning; *bolted* — the only person who bolted was the caller |
| slot3 | romance | *delusion* — a delusion is believed by the person who holds it; she knows hers is invented; *tragedy* — nothing tragic happened |

## One shape conflict, resolved conservatively

`src/screens/challenge.js` renders a people choice under `kind: "portrait"` (it reads
`lesson.people[ref].role`). The current validator accepts `object | person | line | dial` and rejects
`portrait` outright ("unknown kind"), and `person` would fall through the renderer's `default` branch
and print the raw id — which would put a character's name on a portrait, against P0-8.

So **every search challenge uses `kind: "object"`**; no search choice references a person. The reader
still meets portraits in the reconstruct people step, which does not go through `choices`. Decoys are
therefore always "a real object in the wrong role" rather than "a person who was not there" — that
second form is used in the finale instead (the caller's sister, twice).

If someone reconciles the validator and the renderer on `portrait` later, two searches would be
natural to convert: `c8-search` (who carried what across the lawn) and `c10-search` (whose story it is).

## Least-certain items

1. `c3-search` (inference, answer `o-room`). The text says only that *an undefinable something about the
   room* suggested a man lived there, so the correct choice is the parlour itself. It is defensible and
   singular — the window, the letters and the rectory each have a stated owner and role — but it asks the
   reader to pick a room as a "thing", which is the least concrete answer in the lesson.
2. `c1-search`. Excerpt I is the first thing anyone reads, so its three decoys (window, spaniel, carpets)
   are objects the reader has not met yet. They are real story elements and each is wrong for a stateable
   reason, but the item is easier than it looks on paper. Deliberate: it is the warm-up.
3. `c6-search` (birds and duck). Two targets, both on line 77, and the two decoys (carpets, marshes) come
   from the hostess's speech one excerpt earlier — a reader who blurs the two speeches can miss it. That is
   the intended difficulty, but it is the search most likely to be argued with.
4. `c4-search` says "her aunt's husband" rather than "her uncle" — the story never uses the word uncle, and
