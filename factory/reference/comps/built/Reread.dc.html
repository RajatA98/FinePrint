21“Do you know many of the people round here?” asked the
22niece, when she judged that they had had sufficient silent
23communion.
24“Hardly a soul,” said Framton. “My sister was staying
25here, at the rectory, you know, some four years ago, and
26she gave me letters of introduction to some of the people
27here.”
28He made the last statement in a tone of distinct regret.
29“Then you know practically nothing about my aunt?” pursued
30the self-possessed young lady.

Three marks, and two of them are not hers. Twice the narrator tells you what she is — self-possessed, and waiting for her moment. Once, in her own words, she makes certain you cannot check her story. All of it before the tragedy is ever mentioned.

Read it again, now that you know.

The words have not changed. You have.

The first time through you read it at 148 words a minute. This time, 176 — and of those, 144 carried through to something you understood.

You read it faster because you understood it, not the other way round. Neither number is your score.
